The details that decide it

Each of these comes from reviews of other pickers — praised where they work, cursed where they don't.

Zoom loupe that shows every pixel

The magnifier shows the actual bitmap in a 15×15 pixel grid, and the pixel under the crosshair is outlined — you always know which one you're reading.

The values panel stays open after a pick

Pick, compare, pick again. HEX, RGB and HSL stay on screen until you close them — no chasing a tooltip that vanishes.

Auto-copy that really lands in the clipboard

Every pick writes the HEX to your clipboard and confirms it on screen. If the write fails, you see that too — no silent "copied".

Reads your file — not a blurry re-render

Values come from the decoded image data, not from a scaled screenshot of it. What you opened is what you sample.

1 px arrow keys, Enter to pick

Get close with the mouse, land on the exact pixel with the arrows. Enter picks, Esc clears the crosshair.

No ads, no history access, no install warnings

Nothing to dismiss, nothing watching your tabs. The extension asks for no site access at install and runs only where you invoke it.

Questions

How do I pick a color from an image?
Drop the image on this page (or press Ctrl+V), move the loupe to the pixel you want and click. The HEX lands in your clipboard; RGB and HSL sit next to it in the panel.
How do I pick a color from a screenshot?
Take the screenshot as usual, then paste it here with Ctrl+V — no need to save a file first. Screenshots are PNG, so the values you read are exact.
Does it upload my image?
No. The image is decoded inside your browser tab and stays there. Open the DevTools Network panel while you work — nothing is sent.
HEX vs RGB vs HSL — which one do I need?
They are the same color in three notations, and CSS accepts all of them. HEX is the shortest, RGB is what design tools ask for, HSL is the easiest to adjust by hand. The panel shows all three so you copy whichever your tool expects.
Can it pick from a web page, not a file?
This page can't — browsers keep sites out of other tabs. The extension can: it captures the page you invoke it on and picks from the real rendered pixels.
